Summary

The engineering foundation is unusually strong for a game at this stage. A pure, engine‑free rules core, deterministic boards, provably optimal par, driver seams for save, auth, analytics and payments, and genuine accessibility work. That is rare and it is the right base to scale from.

It is not yet ready for a large audience. The blockers are not in the puzzle engine. They are in the economy, in a few player flows, in how level generation behaves on a phone, in missing platform plumbing (Android back button, purchase restore, crash reporting), and in compliance documentation that no longer matches what the build does.

1 · Game logic

A for correctness

What is excellent

  • The rules engine is small, total and argued in comments; the usefulMoves pruning states its cauldron exceptions explicitly.
  • A* over tube‑order‑canonicalised states with two admissible, consistent heuristics. The comment explaining why the cauldron bound must be maxed, not summed, is proof‑in‑code.
  • Deterministic generation per level id, a cheap solvability gate before the exact solve, and a byte‑identical determinism check in CI.
  • No‑win detection is a proof of unwinnability. It removes the most common one‑star review in the genre: “level X is impossible”.
  • The Cauldron is a first‑class rule set. Hashing, pruning, heuristics and UI all understand it.
  • 3,672 checks, including a brute‑force optimality audit under both rule sets and a round‑trip of real support codes.

Findings

L1

Level generation runs on the main thread and is slow for about eight levels High

Measured on this desktop: 180 levels take 50 ms or less, 12 take 50–200 ms, and 8 exceed 200 ms. The worst are L192 at 1.29 s, L92 at 1.11 s, L172 at 0.86 s and L32 at 0.41 s. Mobile Chrome on a mid‑range Android is typically three to six times slower, so “Next level” into L92 or L192 will freeze the UI for several seconds with no feedback. The call is inline in startLevel.

Fix Precompute all 200 boards and solutions at build time into a JSON module (roughly 15 KB gzipped). Generation is deterministic, so the output is identical and the runtime cost drops to zero. It also lets you raise the exact‑solve budget offline so every par is truly optimal. Move whatever still runs at play time (hints, no‑win proofs, endless levels) into a Web Worker.

L2

“Par is genuinely optimal” is not always true Medium

Cauldron levels fall back to a weight‑1.25 search when the exact solve exceeds 60k nodes, and the solver’s optimal flag is never surfaced or asserted for the campaign. The star tolerance absorbs it in practice, but the README claim is stronger than the code.

Fix With precomputed levels the budget constraint disappears. Assert optimal === true for all 200 levels in the core test.

L3

Free powerups nullify the difficulty curve Medium

Every attempt grants three free undos, three hints and three bottles, and a bottle can add up to two tubes. A one‑empty squeeze becomes a three‑empty breather for free, and a hint returns the optimal move, so three stars are trivially reachable on any level. The curve you tuned is optional.

Fix See E2. Market norm is zero or one free hint per level and bottles behind coins or a rewarded ad.

L4

The curve runs out of knobs after level 55 Low

Colours cap at the palette’s eight from level 56 to 200; the remaining dials are minPar (+4 in total) and murky cadence. Players who reach L100 face a hundred near‑identical boards.

Fix Introduce one new idea every 30 levels, the way the cauldron and murk were introduced: five‑unit tubes on some bands, a ninth to twelfth colour (the glyph set has room), a locked tube that opens after N pours, a one‑way tube.

L5

Endless mode is free content you are not shipping Low

The seeded generator already works for any id. “More coming soon” should be an endless mode with a rotating spec, generated in the worker.

L6

Analytics semantics are off Low

The attempt counter is a global session counter, not per level, and level_quit also fires from the win modal’s Home button, polluting the quit funnel. Track attempts per level and emit a single exit event with a reason.

2 · Economy & monetisation

D

This is the largest business risk in the build. The game is fully playable for free, which is right, but there is currently no reason for anyone to pay and one strong reason never to.

E1

Infinite coin farm through replays Critical

Every win pays 50 base plus 25 per star even for a level already cleared, and winning costs no heart. Level 1 has par 4 and takes about ten seconds including animations, paying 125 coins per replay. That is the 500‑coin heart refill in 40 seconds and the equivalent of the $19.99 chest (12,000 coins) in about 16 minutes. Any player who notices never buys anything.

Fix Replays pay nothing, or only the improvement (new stars × 25), or a small flat bounty capped per day.

E2

Too generous to create purchase intent High

1,000 starting coins, 175 per first clear, three free of each powerup per attempt, a hint three‑pack for 200. A player ends the campaign with thousands of coins and nothing to spend them on. Category benchmarks: 0–100 starting coins, one free hint per level, bottles never free.

Fix Start at 200 coins; free uses of one undo, one hint, no bottle (unlimited undo is defensible since it reduces frustration rather than selling anything); first clear 50 + 15 per star. Make RemoteConfig actually fetch so this can be tuned live. The seam already exists.

E3

No rewarded video High

For casual puzzle at scale, rewarded ads are typically the majority of revenue and the main free‑to‑play currency: watch for a hint, a bottle, a heart, double coins. The release checklist declares no ads. That is a legitimate choice, but hearts plus IAP‑only is the friction model of Candy Crush without its scale.

Fix Decide. If no ads, remove hearts (E6). If ads, put them behind an AdsService driver exactly like Payments, and redo the data‑safety forms.

E4

Purchases can be paid for and never granted High

The Play Billing driver completes the payment sheet and grants in the same call. If the app dies between the sheet closing and the coins landing, the player has paid, received nothing, and because the token was never consumed the SKU cannot be bought again. There is no listPurchases() on boot.

Fix On init, list purchases, grant every unconsumed token idempotently (persist granted tokens in the save), then consume. Reorder the live purchase to grant, persist token, consume, then complete. Mirror this in the future StoreKit driver.

E5

Receipt validation, and a cheap anti‑cheat you already own Medium

Client‑side grants are spoofable; a small verification endpoint is needed before revenue matters. Worth noting for the roadmap: because boards are deterministic, a server can verify any claimed solve by replaying the move list. That is exact, cheap anti‑cheat for a future leaderboard without trusting the client.

E6

Heart rules are harsh and inconsistent Medium

A heart is lost on quit, not only on failure, and on Restart from the stuck dialog, while the top‑bar restart button is free and stays available after a “no way to win” toast. The top water‑sort games generally have no lives at all.

Fix Either drop hearts and gate with ads or coins, or lose a heart only on failure and dead‑end restart, never on quit, with every restart path consistent.

E7

Heart regeneration trusts the device clock Low

Changing the system time refills hearts. Acceptable now; note it for the server milestone.

3 · UI / UX

B

What is excellent

  • Coherent candy visual language; HUD in DOM for crisp text, Pixi only where it earns its cost.
  • The tutorial advances on real actions and the hand points at the solver’s actual next move.
  • Tapping a non‑target that could be a source switches selection instead of buzzing. Small, and exactly right.
  • Pour choreography: level liquid surface under tilt, parabolic stream, amount‑pitched audio, input reopening before the return arc.
  • Accessibility: colour‑blind glyphs, reduced motion, 44 px hit areas, dialog roles, focus moved into dialogs, live‑region toasts, safe‑area insets, desktop keyboard.

Findings

U1

Android back button exits the app High

There is no history handling anywhere. In a Trusted Web Activity, back on the game screen, in the shop, or with a modal open closes the whole app.

Fix Push a history entry per screen and per modal; on popstate close the modal or return home; on home, let it exit. Web players get a working browser back for free.

U2

No mid‑level resume High

Closing the app mid‑level discards the board and history. Casual players background games constantly.

Fix Persist level id, board, history, hidden units, extra tubes, uses and start time on every move (writes are already coalesced) and offer “Continue level 47?” on launch.

U3

Stranded after a win with zero hearts High

Win, tap Next level, startLevel sees no hearts and opens the lives dialog over the solved board. Close it and the player is standing on a completed level with a live back button. Back then warns “You will also lose a heart” for a level already won, and the quit path calls breakStreak unconditionally, so the win streak is broken by winning. Restarting that board also re‑awards coins (E1).

Fix When the lives dialog closes without a retry on a resolved board, go home. Break the streak only when a heart is actually lost. Treat back on a resolved board as plain navigation.

U4

Every launch costs 1.6 seconds of splash Medium

The splash is a fixed‑duration animation that boot awaits, regardless of how fast boot actually was. Returning players should be on home well under a second.

Fix Drive the bar by real progress with a short minimum around half a second so it never flashes.

U5

No retention loop Medium

The only reason to come back is the heart timer. All of the following work without a server because boards are deterministic: a daily challenge seeded by the date with a streak calendar; a daily login reward and seven‑day chest; achievements built on the lifetime stats that already exist; a heart‑refill notification from the native wrapper.

U6

The level map is a flat 200‑node grid Medium

Competitors organise into themed chapters every 20–25 levels with a completion celebration and a change of scenery. The level names, palette and mechanic cadence already give natural chapter boundaries; this is mostly presentation.

U7

Music starts on the first tap anywhere Medium

Common, and a frequent cause of mutes and one‑star reviews. Consider fading music in after the tutorial’s first pour, or defaulting it off on the web while keeping it on in the installed app.

U8

Twist mechanics are introduced by a toast only Medium

A four‑second toast at the moment a new vessel appears is easy to miss on the first cauldron (L22) and first murky level (L36). Reuse the tutorial machinery for a two‑step guided intro on those levels.

U9

Accessibility gaps Low

Modals have no focus trap, so Tab escapes to the background. user-scalable=no with maximum-scale=1 violates WCAG 1.4.4; consider allowing zoom on DOM‑only screens. Shop badges rely on colour alone.

U10

Every string is hard‑coded English Low

Roughly 120 strings across main.ts, index.html and the tutorial. Introduce a string table now; retrofitting later is far more expensive.

U11

Toasts have no queue or cap Low

The cauldron intro, a no‑win warning and the “Stuck? Try a hint” nudge can stack on the same board.

4 · Security, integrity & compliance

B

Threat model today: no backend, the client owns all state, players can only cheat themselves. That is the right model for single‑player, and it stops being adequate the moment anything competitive or paid depends on client state.

S1

Production sourcemaps High

sourcemap: true ships 2.7 MB of maps that reconstruct every source file verbatim, including the support‑code HMAC secret. Forging a self‑targeted “coins 65535” or “level 200” code goes from reversing a minified bundle to reading a file.

Fix Set sourcemap: false, or 'hidden' and upload maps only to your error tracker. It is also a bandwidth and cache win.

S2

Privacy and compliance drift Medium

The analytics module ships a live PostHog token and sends every event with the save’s support ID as the distinct id. The release checklist still says “no data collected, analytics are local/console today”, and the README says analytics log to the console in dev. For Play’s Data Safety form and Apple’s label this is app interactions plus device or other IDs, collected, not shared. For EU and UK users a pseudonymous analytics ID generally needs a disclosure and a lawful basis.

Fix Update both docs, write the privacy policy, add a “Share anonymous usage data” toggle in Settings, and restrict the PostHog project to your domains.

S3

No Content Security Policy Medium

GitHub Pages cannot set headers, but a meta CSP works and turns any future injected script into a no‑op. Cheap insurance for a game that will handle payments.

Fix default-src 'self'; script-src 'self'; connect-src 'self' https://us.i.posthog.com; img-src 'self' data: blob:; style-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'; worker-src 'self' blob:

S4

Supply chain hygiene Medium

Only two runtime dependencies, lockfile committed, npm ci in CI. Good. Add npm audit --omit=dev to the workflow, enable Dependabot, and pin the GitHub Actions to SHAs.

S5

Save data is trusted on load Low

Migration trusts shapes. A hand‑edited or corrupted record with a non‑numeric star count propagates NaN into the totals and the home screen. Validate numerically and drop bad entries.

S6

Payment completes before the grant Low

The Play sheet is completed with success before the grant and before consume; a throw after completion loses the purchase. Covered by the E4 reorder.

S7

XSS surface reviewed and clean Info

The only untrusted string is the profile name; it is escaped where innerHTML is used and set with textContent elsewhere. Dialog markup is app‑authored only. Support codes are fully validated before use.

S8

Support codes are proportionate Info

48‑bit truncated HMAC, device‑bound, single‑use, 14‑day expiry. The documented trade‑off of a client‑side secret is fine as long as S1 is fixed and codes never gate anything competitive.

Hint and no‑win checks from live, solvable positions cost under a millisecond even on the hardest levels; the weighted search finds a line immediately. The only expensive solver case is the proof of unwinnability, which is budget‑capped. Level generation is the real cost (L1).

P1

Main‑thread level generation High

Same finding as L1. Precompute plus a worker.

P2

About 0.9 MB before the first interactive frame Medium

Most of it is images preloaded at high priority. The logo is 299 KB for a slot at most 360 CSS pixels wide.

Fix Re‑encode the logo at quality around 75 (70–90 KB) or ship AVIF with WebP fallback; Entry can drop to about 120 KB. Defer the home art until the splash is showing. Consider lazy‑loading the Pixi chunk until the game screen, since splash, profile, home, map and shop are pure DOM.

P3

The render loop never pauses off the game screen Medium

setPaused is only called on visibility change. On home, map and shop the canvas is hidden but the ticker still updates the starfield, particles and every bottle each frame. Pure battery and thermal cost, and the “modal pauses rendering” comment in GameStage is not true.

Fix Pause when the current screen is not the game and while a modal is open.

P4

Per‑frame Graphics rebuilds Low

Starfield and particles clear and rebuild a Graphics every frame. Fine at 90 stars and 420 particles; on low‑end GPUs a sprite starfield or a static scrolling texture is cheaper. Measure first.

P5

Antialiasing at resolution 2 Low

MSAA on a 2× buffer is the most expensive default Pixi offers on phones. At high DPR the glass edges are already smooth. Consider antialias only below DPR 2, and profile on a Pixel 4a or Galaxy A14 class device before submission.

P6

Backdrop blur on every dialog Low

A 7 px full‑screen blur on each modal open is a visible hitch on low‑end Android. Fall back to a solid tint under reduced motion, or drop the blur.

P7

Service worker cache grows forever Low

The cache version is constant and activation only deletes other cache names, so each deploy’s hashed assets accumulate. Inject the build hash at build time, or prune on activate to the precache plus the assets the current index references.

Worth keeping: the transparent canvas over a CSS gradient, the delta clamp on resume, dirty‑flag liquid redraws, coalesced save writes with a flush on pagehide, manual chunking and module preloads.

6 · Reliability, observability & ops

C+
O1

No error telemetry High

No window.onerror, no unhandled‑rejection handler, no exception capture. With millions of sessions you will meet WebGL context losses, storage quota errors, Pixi init failures on odd GPUs and unknown unknowns, and you will hear about them only in reviews.

Fix Global handlers that send renderer, level, save version, support ID and a stack to PostHog exception capture or Sentry, plus a friendly recovery UI.

O2

Boot failure leaves the splash at 100 % forever Medium

If the Pixi stage fails to initialise, boot rejects and nothing is shown. Catch it and offer Reload, or fall back to the canvas renderer.

O3

Analytics loses offline events Medium

Each event is a fire‑and‑forget fetch. Queue to storage, flush on reconnect, and batch.

O4

End‑to‑end tests do not run in CI Medium

The smoke test hard‑codes Edge on Windows. Switch to Playwright’s bundled Chromium so the flow test guards every deploy, and consider PR previews instead of deploying every push to main straight to production.

O5

Two tabs, one save Low

Last write wins silently. Listen for the storage event and reload or warn.

O6

No build version in the app Low

Inject the git SHA at build and show it in Settings. It is the first thing support needs.
